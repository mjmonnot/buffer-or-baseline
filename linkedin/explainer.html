Destatis, 22 April 2026: 25% of German employees still worked from home in 2025, against 13% in 2019. This year’s isolation and return-to-office studies disagree on location. They do not show that the office, by itself, fixes loneliness.

Buffer or Baseline?

Trait Self-Compassion and Employee Well-Being Across Disruption, Telework, and Digital Demands in a National Probability Panel

GESIS Panel.pop · employed in 2018 · N = 3,052

A 2018 self-compassion score set where people stood. People a standard deviation higher entered 2020 with life satisfaction about 3% higher, and six years later they were less lonely and less overwhelmed by digital tools. Through the 2020–2021 drop, life satisfaction fell about 9% at every level of the trait, and through the telework years everyone moved at the same rate. The trait marked the level, and the level is what lasted.

2018

Self-compassion measured before COVID-19. The resource is already in hand.

2020–2021

Life satisfaction fell about 9% of its 2020 level. High and low on the trait each lost 9%, and the high group stayed about 6% above the low group.

2021–2025

Telework share moved with life satisfaction the same way at every level of the trait.

2024

The 2018 score was associated with loneliness six years on — the largest mark in the study.

The picture

Implied life satisfaction (0–100) at ±1 SD of the 2018 score. Each SD of the trait added 3% to the 2020 level (b = 2.16). The drop that followed was 9% at both ends (b for the slope = −0.10, BF01 = 51.52).

In plain terms · r = −.36

Of 100 employees in the upper half on 2018 self-compassion, 68 were in the less-lonely half six years later. Of 100 in the lower half, 32 were.

Three numbers, one scale

One SD higher on the 2018 score, and the 2024 outcome is expected this much lower.

Self-caring items, entered alongside: β = +.01. After a six-item neuroticism control the self-critical estimates were −.24 and −.17.
