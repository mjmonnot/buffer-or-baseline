Key references

The papers this study is in conversation with.

Two lists. The first places the result. The second is how this companion is drawn. Full scholarly citations are in the preprint.

The result

  • Monnot, M. J. (2026). Buffer or baseline? Trait self-compassion and employee well-being across disruption, telework, and digital demands in a national probability panel. SSRN. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7622018 The paper this site accompanies.
  • Hobfoll, S. E. (1989). Conservation of resources: A new attempt at conceptualizing stress. American Psychologist, 44(3), 513–524. The resource claim the three tests were written to evaluate: a held resource should mark level and attenuate loss.
  • Yap, S. C. Y., Anusic, I., & Lucas, R. E. (2012). Does personality moderate reaction and adaptation to major life events? Evidence from the British Household Panel Survey. Journal of Research in Personality, 46(5), 477–488. The levels-versus-change dissociation this study extends from the Big Five and personal events.
  • Anusic, I., Yap, S. C. Y., & Lucas, R. E. (2014). Does personality moderate reaction and adaptation to major life events? Analysis of life satisfaction and affect in an Australian national sample. Journal of Research in Personality, 51, 69–77. Replication of that null in a second national panel.
  • Neff, K. D. (2003). Self-compassion: An alternative conceptualization of a healthy attitude toward oneself. Self and Identity, 2(2), 85–101. The construct: self-kindness versus self-judgment, common humanity versus isolation, mindfulness versus over-identification.
  • Raes, F., Pommier, E., Neff, K. D., & Van Gucht, D. (2011). Construction and factorial validation of a short form of the Self-Compassion Scale. Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy, 18(3), 250–255. The short form administered in the panel.
  • Muris, P., & Petrocchi, N. (2017). Protection or vulnerability? A meta-analysis of the relations between the positive and negative components of self-compassion and psychopathology. Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy, 24(2), 373–383. Why the total score can inflate associations: the negative components carry more of the covariance. This panel finds the same split for loneliness and digital overwhelm.
  • Wanberg, C. R., Csillag, B., Douglass, R. P., Zhou, L., & Pollard, M. S. (2020). Socioeconomic status and well-being during COVID-19: A resource-based examination. Journal of Applied Psychology, 105(12), 1382–1396. A U.S. probability panel in the same period: more education did not buffer the decline.
  • GESIS. (2026). GESIS Panel.pop Standard Edition (ZA5665, Version 62.0.0) [Data set]. https://doi.org/10.4232/1.14772 The archive. Microdata are not redistributed here.

How this site is drawn

Numbers stay on the marks. One navy accent, on the association that was found rather than hypothesized. A documentation line on every graphic. No frames, no 3-D, no decorative color. The LinkedIn card is the same drawing, not a screenshot of the paper figures.

  • Tufte, E. R. (1992). Citation Classic commentary on The visual display of quantitative information. Data-ink. Above all else show the data. Graphics as datawords, not boxed occasions.
  • Powsner, S. M., & Tufte, E. R. (1997). Summarizing clinical psychiatric data. Psychiatric Services, 48(11), 1458–1461. A compact longitudinal plot that sits next to the numbers it summarizes.
  • Bostock, M., Ogievetsky, V., & Heer, J. (2011). D³: Data-Driven Documents. IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics, 17(12), 2301–2309. https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2011.185 Marks are data. Interaction, where it exists, interrogates a published coefficient, not a decoration.
  • Lupi, G. (2017, January 30). Data humanism, the revolution will be visualized. Print. The N is employed adults, not a stock photo of “workers.” The site does not host respondent-level files.
  • Cox, A. (2012, June 1). Interview with Amanda Cox. Simply Statistics. Annotation on the mark. The sentence is on the graphic, not in a caption that restates it.
  • Knaflic, C. N. (2017, August 9). My guiding principles. storytellingwithdata.com. One claim per panel. Declutter. Highlight only the found association.
  • Cairo, A. (2019–2022). Selected posts at thefunctionalart.blogspot.com. Function first. The overlapping intervals are the point of the disruption panel.
  • McCandless, D. (2010, July). The beauty of data visualization [TED talk]. Relative scale. The three paths share one drop; the later loneliness mark is larger because the association is larger.
  • Few, S. Perceptual Edge white papers. Position and length over angle or area. Cleveland dots, not pies or 3-D bars.
